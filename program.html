Where 1,700 Comes From

Maintenance is roughly what you burn in a day — this number already factors in lifting 4–5x a week plus the daily steps/cardio below. Cut 700 under that, and here's what it adds up to:

~2,400 − 1,700 = 700/day deficit
700 × 7 days
Weekly deficit 4,900 cal
4,900 ÷ 3,500cal/lb fat
≈ 1.4 lb / week

Over 12 weeks (3 months), that's 16.8 lb — the ceiling, if every day stays food-only with zero drinks.

10,000+ steps a day, plus real cardio

Treadmill, elliptical, stairmaster, or just walking more — 20–30 min, a few times a week, stacked on top of hitting 10k steps daily outside the gym. That activity is already baked into the ~2,400 maintenance number above; skip it and there's less deficit left to work with.

What About Drinking?

A shot is about 97 cal, and it lands on top of your food, not instead of it — the 1,700 above is food, every day, drinking or not. So drinking doesn't change what you eat, it eats into the weekly deficit above instead:

15 drinks × 97cal ≈ 1,500/wk
4,900 − 1,500 = 3,400/wk
÷ 3,500 cal/lb ≈ 1 lb / week

Over 12 weeks, that's about 12 lb — still a real deficit, just a touch slower than the 16.8 lb ceiling with zero drinks.

Doesn't have to split evenly across the weekend — lean 10 into one night and the other stays lighter, around 5. Beer and mixed drinks run higher than a plain shot, so the count drops if that's what you're actually having. Food still stays above 1,500 a day regardless — that's a separate rule for protein, unrelated to the math above.

04

If You Slip Up

No panic, no crash day

Went over one night? Don't try to fix it in a single day. Drop intake by 50–150 calories/day across the rest of the week until it's made up. A 400-calorie overshoot is only about 60/day spread across the next week — barely noticeable, and it protects the muscle you're trying to keep.
