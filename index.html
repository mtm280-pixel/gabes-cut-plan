What is BMR?

BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) is how many calories your body burns just existing — breathing, pumping blood, keeping you alive — if you laid in bed all day and did absolutely nothing. It's the floor, not the whole picture. Everything you actually do on top of that (walking, lifting, cardio, even fidgeting) burns more on top of it.

It's calculated from exact age, height, and weight — not a generic guess — using the Mifflin-St Jeor formula, the same one most clinical calculators use.

03

Staying Active

Movement matters beyond the gym — it's what makes the deficit sustainable without starving. Pick whichever Gabe will actually do consistently:

  • Elliptical or StairMaster — easiest on the joints, high calorie burn for the effort. Personal favorite.
  • Treadmill, 30 min — incline walk or light jog.
  • Just walk more — a lot. No gym required, still counts.

The specific machine doesn't matter much. Consistency does — 30 min most days beats an hour once a week.

04

Eating Strategy

Staying in a calorie target is easy on paper and hard in practice when food doesn't fill you up. The fix: build meals around high-volume, high-protein, low-calorie food — big portions, real taste, low cost in calories.

Go-to examples

High-protein low-cal pizza and burgers are two staples that work — happy to walk through how to make both. The exact foods matter less than finding a small rotation that works.

The real trick: find 2–3 meals Gabe actually likes that fit the calories, and lean on them on repeat. Variety is nice, but consistency is what actually gets results — trying to reinvent every meal is the #1 reason people fall off. Send over his favorite foods and they can be rebuilt into high-volume, lower-calorie versions.

What You're Getting

This isn't a plan that gets handed over once and forgotten. Two things keep it on track:

A real diet plan — not a generic template

A written plan built around Gabe's actual numbers (calorie target + a protein target high enough to hold onto muscle while the fat comes off) and his real favorite foods, rebuilt into high-volume, high-protein versions he'll actually want to eat. Not a cookie-cutter 1,800-calorie PDF everyone gets.

Weekly check-ins

Every week: review the weight trend (the average, not one noisy day), how adherence actually went, energy and hunger levels, and anything that's getting in the way. From there the calorie target gets adjusted up or down based on how his body is actually responding — not left on autopilot for 12 weeks. That's also the moment to troubleshoot plateaus, bad weeks, or travel/social stuff before they derail the whole plan.

Direct access in between too — if something's not working or a question comes up mid-week, that doesn't wait for the next check-in.
